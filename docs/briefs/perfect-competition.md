# <Engagement> — engagement brief

## The problem
The farmer needs to decide the most appropriate mix of vegetables in the 64 planting beds beds to maximize the profitability of the farm. Each of the vegetables will yield a different price at market. Each type of vegetable requires different amounts of care and will incur different costs to grow it. They all have different rates of diminishing returns that I will assume means that some are more difficult to store and ship.

If the choice of vegetables is poor then you will not maximize the profit for the farm. Worse, if it is particularly bad, the farm will lose money and possibly go out of business.

Mesclun is the middle most crop in difficulty and expense to grow and harvest and brings in the same high price as tomatoes. It has the lowest rate of diminishing returns. Carrots are the least difficult and expensive to grow and have the rate of diminishing return in the middle of the group, but brings in the lowest amount of money at market. Tomatoes are also profitable but require the highest tending and harvesting costs along with having the highest rate of diminishing returns.

## What I am assuming
The amount of work, cost of fertilizer, diminishing returns, and price at market are all given in advance. The farm has a fixed spending limit, fixed number of beds, and fixed hours of farm labor of various types.

If I had more time and more data I would like to see the crops that the farmer had planted in previous years and how profitable each mix was. I would also be concerned about the weather predictions for the upcoming year to help decide what crops are more likely to thrive. (I think we all remember the bad pumpkin crops in fall because the summer had been much too rainy in 2009, 2015, and 2018.) The effects of climate change would also be a factor worth investigating.

## Hypothesis
I believe the mix should include all three types of vegetables. I would recommend the following:
32 beds of Mesclun
16 beds of Carrots
16 beds of Tomatoes

I would recommend mesclun be the greater part of the planting because is has a low diminishing return rate and a high price at market with a middling cost of production. I would recommend equal parts of carrots and tomatoes because they seem to have fairly well matched advantages and disadvantages. Carrots have a lower price at market and lower diminishing return rate but also lower costs associated with care and harvesting. Tomatoes require the largest expenditure on care and harvesting and have the highest diminishing returns rate, but bring in a high price at market.

## How I would know I was wrong
I will know if I was wrong if the farm does not maximize the profit from this selection. I will know I was very wrong if the farm does not make any profit at all or if it loses money.
